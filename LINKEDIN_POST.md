Gardening leave, and I still can't switch off.

Every year, a group of us do a "marathon" pub crawl — a full day walking the river from Richmond to Greenwich, with a proper string of stops along the way. It's become a real tradition. This year it doubles as my partner's 30th, at the end of August, so instead of planning it the normal way, I built him a proper app for it.

A live tracker that shows the group where we are, what pub's next, and when we're moving on. Everyone can rate each pub, log their drinks, and get a shareable recap of the day at the end.

Built solo, evenings only, pairing with an AI coding assistant to move a lot faster than I would have alone — the kind of thing that would've taken a small team a sprint, done over a handful of nights.

This past Friday, rather than jumping straight into the full 20-mile version, we ran a smaller test crawl around some fantastic classic pubs in Greenwich with a brilliant group of friends — a proper dry run of the app with real people, before it has to hold up on the actual day. A few bugs only showed up once real people were using it live (one would've quietly shown everyone the wrong pub arrival times) — fixed and redeployed to production on the spot, mid-crawl.

It was a good reminder that testing with real users beats testing in your head every time, whether that's software or a day out. You can plan for every scenario, but nothing surfaces the actual problems like watching real people use the thing you built.

That, alongside staying properly hands-on with the tech itself, has been the best part of this bit of time away from work.

The real crawl's still a few weeks out. Friday was the testing ground. 🍺
